# 033 — Dense steampunk districts

## Accepted direction

Larger cities should have industrial, residential and commercial districts. The user rejected detached cottages in repeated spacious blocks, requesting New York-like packed frontages and an overdeveloped, crowded steampunk character. Some areas should remain maintained while residential/service/industrial spaces accumulate litter and storage. They requested additional model variety, construction sites, cranes, high walkways, Victorian silhouettes, building illumination and industrial smoke.

## Implemented in the experiment

The city diorama defaults to a 160-building district study. The 28-building composition remains selectable. Pure district planning grows serviced blocks around the civic neighborhood, keeps industry beside the lower quay, places commercial frontages near the core and residential blocks farther back, and validates footprint/street clearance and level sites. Block counts account for the exact requested building budget. Collector extents follow actual occupied blocks.

The urban kit adds narrow, deep three-story buildings, storefronts, red-brick variants, steep roof variants, rooftop utilities and scaffolded construction shells. Seeded height variation breaks uniform rooflines. Urban lots are packed eight per frontage with small lateral gaps and a narrow shared rear alley. Larger factory models retain industrial yards. Material-batched clutter differentiates cleaner commercial blocks from grimier residential and industrial alleys. Selected industrial yards have stylized cranes; selected paired residential frontages have elevated walkways across the rear alley.

Warm urban windows use emissive materials, not actual scene lights. Industrial smoke uses one instanced mesh capped at 48 puffs from 12 chimneys, no shadows and no physics. These effects are decorative; no gameplay authority changes.

## Provisional and future

This is a bounded district generator on the study's prescribed elevation and waterfront, not automatic zoning of arbitrary campaign terrain. Current district dimensions, mix, urban heights, clutter, cranes and walkway frequency are art tuning. Block silhouettes still follow a rectangular framework; more corner buildings, irregular parcels, mills and district transitions remain needed. The seed changes variants/heights, not the underlying street framework. District summaries count expansion buildings; the 28-building civic neighborhood is separate. No construction simulation, walkable elevated paths, shadow-casting window lights or smoke simulation is implied. Older city measurements do not validate these new assets.
